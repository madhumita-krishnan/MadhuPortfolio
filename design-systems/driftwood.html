Dashboard Product

Home shows how the demos are doing, and the detail sits one press away. A white menu column on a panel-blue page, with white widgets on a soft lift.

The menu: twelve items to six

Clients said the old dashboard overwhelmed them. The menu now holds what people come for, actions first.

  1. Home, Approvals, Inbox, Strategy, Demos, Face & Voice. Grouped by what you came to do. A tab names one place, with no second row of segments.
  2. Approvals counts what waits, so what needs you is answered before a click.
  3. Face & Voice stays until it's recorded, then moves under Demos.
  4. Set-up lives behind your name at the menu's foot: sending accounts, team, assets, integrations.
  5. The menu is the frame. Logo on top, you at the foot, held in view as the page scrolls, with no header bar. Names in title case.

Home follows what clients asked the dashboard for, in the order they asked, one section each.

Approvals

Triggers

  1. Each trigger is its own widget: its name, leads found, and what it watches on hover.
  2. Swipe left to delete. "Delete" shows behind the card as it's pulled; a short pull springs back; Undo waits five seconds.
  3. Add where the list ends: the + sits centred at the foot of the column, and its field opens there.

    Interaction patterns Product

    The principles behind every screen, each learned from a real miss across 66 logged iterations. People act with confidence when every step is safe.

    Undo right where it happenedA costly action shows at once and its button becomes Undo for a few seconds. Adding is free to take back.
    Instant responseThe screen reacts straight away and the real work happens quietly. "Disconnecting…" for five seconds felt slow.
    Two choices at most at the bottomOne main button and one quiet way back. Back sits bottom left and the way on bottom right, in one place on every step.
    One way onTwo controls that do the same thing is one too many. The way forward appears once it's possible.
    Everything holds stillRegions keep their size whatever they hold. A frame swaps its contents and keeps its corners and shadow. The menu stays put.
    Calm, restrained motionWords soften out, then the object moves, then new words come in. Each turn moves its own way; letters and faces never spin.
    Never strand people in another tabA sign-in that can't redirect back opens as a small window over the page and lands you where you were.
    Delight lives in the sea, sparinglyThe log sails while a demo is made; the first Publish gets fireworks, a bottle and a splash. The cast is water creatures only.
    A popup you open staysUntil ×, Escape or a click outside, so it can be read and scrolled. A panel that slides in can be swiped away.
    Secondary looks secondaryOnly the way on looks recommended. A decision is quiet and whole: a raised white thumb, a tide wash and a tick.
    Respect a skipA skipped step is never asked for again. Heavy asks, like recording a face, come later by email.
    Every button has its whole journeyRest, hover, pressed, keyboard focus, disabled, and busy, its label saying so: "Opening…".

    Site rules

    One voice phrase per headingEvery site heading carries one Georgia-italic phrase in tide, on the phrase that carries the meaning, and never two.
    No em dashes in site copyThe one exception is the slop parody card. Slop uses em dashes; that's the joke.
    Break lines on meaningtext-wrap: balance can split a phrase across lines, so bound phrases get an &nbsp; and the turn lands where the sense turns.
    Real people, real numbersNumbers are stated as observed fact with attribution. Real names appear only with consent; otherwise redaction bars.
    Quality floor, unannouncedResponsive · visible keyboard focus · reduced motion respected · every interactive element has hover, active, focus and disabled · data has loading, empty and error.
